What we are tracking

Ask an assistant where to buy handcrafted gemstone jewelry in Sedona and it now answers with your Sedona page, by name. Google has that same page sitting on about page seven.

There are two different doors into your shop now, and they no longer open together.

For years being findable meant one thing: showing up high in Google’s list of blue links. That still matters, and plenty of your customers shop exactly that way.

But a growing number never see that list. They ask an assistant a question in plain English and get back one answer, with a few shops named in it. Being named in that answer is a different thing from showing up high in Google’s list, and the two have come apart. Being strong in one does not carry you in the other.

That is why this section has two parts. The first asks: do assistants name you? The second asks: where do you sit in Google’s list? Your Sedona page is currently a very clean example of one door wide open and the other shut.

Four words used a lot below, in plain terms. Being named is when an assistant mentions you in its answer. A listing is one time your page appeared in somebody’s Google results. Your spot is how far down that Google list you were, where 1st to 10th is the first page and almost nobody scrolls past it. A click from Google is somebody seeing your listing and choosing to open it. Comparison pages are pages we deliberately left alone, so we can tell your results apart from the weather.

One honest limit, and it matters for reading that zero. This table can only see Google. A zero in the last column means nobody opened that page from a Google results page. It does not mean nobody reached you. When an assistant names you and somebody comes straight to your shop, Google never sees that visit at all. Some of those people click straight through and your own site analytics catch them, but plenty read the answer and then simply type your name into their browser, which arrives looking identical to somebody who already knew you. This is genuinely hard to count, and anybody who says they can measure it precisely is selling something. What it means in practice is that the effect of being named is almost certainly larger than any number here can show, not smaller.

The big one: same page, one door open and one shut. An assistant now recommends your Sedona page as the strongest match for somebody shopping for handcrafted gemstone jewelry in Sedona. Google has that same page at about the 69th spot, shown 7 times in four weeks. Somebody who asks an assistant finds it. Somebody who types into Google effectively cannot.

What that tells us about the next move. The page itself is clearly good enough, because an assistant reads it and picks it. What it lacks is the thing Google weighs most heavily and assistants weigh less: other places on the web pointing at it. That is the gap to close, and it is the reason the next moves below are about links rather than more writing.

Your name is working hard. Your home page brings in 38 of your 45 clicks at around the 4th spot. People who already know you find you easily. The work we are doing is aimed at the people who do not know you yet, which is a slower and harder thing to move.

And one honest caution, because you should not take a good month at face value. One of the comparison pages, the Passion Flower necklace, also started being named this month, and we never touched that page. When a page we left alone improves too, some of the change is the assistants getting better rather than the work landing. So I am calling this month a direction and not a proven result. What makes me hopeful is the shape of it: your gains are specific to the exact page we built, and the comparison gain is a general one. If the Sedona result holds next month, that settles it. These are small numbers either way, and they are read across several months, never one.
